The short version

I learned the customer from inside the chair.

I started in the operatory. As a Registered Dental Hygienist in high-volume Brooklyn and Staten Island practices, I learned exactly how a dental office runs — what makes a clinician trust a new tool, and what makes them quietly abandon it.

Then I took that fluency into the field. At Align Technology I converted 300+ practices to digital scanning workflows. At OraPharma (Bausch Health) I ranked top 10 nationally in commercial sales, growing my territory roughly 10% a quarter. Now at Dandy, I help practices retire the analog workflow for good: digital scans, an AI-supported lab, and fewer hours lost to remakes and reseats.

That path gives me a view most people only get from one side. I've been the hygienist told to "just use the new scanner," and the rep asking the practice to change. Companies bring me in to understand how dental offices actually buy, adopt, and operate: where the schedule breaks, who holds the real veto, and why good products end up in a closet.

How I help companies

Where dental offices get stuck, and how to work with them anyway.

01

Chair time is the currency

Every new tool competes with production. If it adds minutes to a seat appointment, it loses, no matter how good the demo was.

02

The doctor isn't the only buyer

Office managers own scheduling, billing, and vendors. Assistants own the scanner. Skip them and a signed deal stalls on day one.

03

Hidden costs live in the schedule

Remakes, adjustments, and reseat visits rarely show up on a P&L. I help teams surface them in the practice's own numbers.

04

Payer mix shapes every decision

What insurance reimburses decides what a practice can afford to change. Pricing and positioning have to start there.

05

Adoption beats activation

Plenty of practices own technology they don't use. Training, workflow fit, and a first quick win decide whether it sticks.

06

Trust travels by word of mouth

Dentists talk. One bad-fit account costs more than one lost deal, so qualifying honestly is a growth strategy.
